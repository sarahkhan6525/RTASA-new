RTASA — plain-English walkthrough

🔐 About the codes you'll see ("OTP")

OTP = One-Time Password — a 6-digit code that changes every 30 seconds, the same idea as a banking app's login code. Every action below fetches a fresh one automatically and uses it to prove "this is really Owner / Custodian 1 / etc., right now" — on top of their password. The actual code is shown every time it's used, below.

Or go step-by-step yourself below — every button is real, nothing is pre-recorded.

Step 1 — Create the people involved

One owner of the secret, three trusted custodians who will each hold a piece of the key, and one requester who will ask to see the secret later. Each gets their own password and security code — nothing is shared between them.

Step 2 — Lock up a secret

Write anything you like below. When you click the button: it gets encrypted, the encryption key gets split into 3 pieces (any 2 will be enough later), and each piece gets hidden inside an ordinary-looking image.

Step 3 — Ask permission to unlock it

The requester asks to see the secret. Nothing happens until at least 2 of the 3 custodians agree — click "Approve" for each one to simulate them agreeing.

Step 4 — Prove the history can't be secretly changed

Every action above was written into a tamper-evident log. First let's look at it, then prove it's genuinely tamper-evident — not just "trust me."

That's the whole system.

Encryption, a threshold of people who must agree, keys hidden in plain sight, and a history that can't be quietly rewritten — all four working together, for real, against the real backend running on this machine. Click "Start over" above to run it again.
